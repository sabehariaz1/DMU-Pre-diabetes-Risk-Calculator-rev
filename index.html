A score above 5 means several common risk factors for pre-diabetes are present. This is not a diagnosis, but it is a strong reason to talk with a healthcare provider about blood glucose testing (such as an A1C or fasting glucose test) in the near future.

Steps that can help lower your risk

  • •Schedule a check-up and ask specifically about pre-diabetes or diabetes screening.
  • •Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate activity per week (e.g., brisk walking), spread across most days.
  • •If you carry excess weight, losing even 5–7% of your body weight measurably lowers risk.
  • •Reduce refined carbohydrates and sugary drinks; favor vegetables, whole grains, and lean protein.
  • •Get 7–9 hours of sleep and address chronic stress, both of which affect blood sugar regulation.
  • •If a parent or sibling has type 2 diabetes, mention this explicitly to your provider — it changes screening frequency.

General wellness tips

  • •Keep a regular movement routine — walking, cycling, or swimming most days of the week.
  • •Build meals around vegetables, whole grains, and lean protein, with sugary drinks as an occasional treat.
  • •Keep an eye on your waist size and weight over time — small early changes are easiest to reverse.
  • •Mention your family history at your next routine physical, even if your own risk looks low today.
  • •Re-take this screening yearly, or sooner if your weight, activity, or health history changes.
